Round 3: training on the expanded set

Result: more and more varied training data barely moves the scores. We trained ESRT on the v2 set: 307 training tiles against 200 in round 1, with the new tiles mostly arid inland ground. It used the paper's loss (SSIM plus histogram matching), 8,000 steps at batch 16, with and without the change mask. On validation the two seed-0 runs tied (0.4170 without the mask, 0.4168 with it), so the unmasked model got the second seed (0.4164). Every model scores in the round 1 model's normalization.

v2 against the round 1 model, old holdouts. Across the 152 old held-out tiles (Houston, Owens, Western Australia and the paper's test scenes), the v2 model changes SSIM against Lee-filtered Stripmap by +0.13 points with seed 0 (p = 0.065) and +0.03 with seed 1 (p = 0.23). Neither change is significant. Two regions move the same way under both seeds, in opposite directions:

  • Western Australia, an arid and sparse holdout, gains +0.55 and +0.52 points, with 98% and 92% of tiles better (p < 10−9). This matches the added desert training ground.
  • Houston, a dense city, loses −0.14 and −0.29 points, with 8% and 0% of tiles better (p < 10−9).
Owens shows no consistent change (+0.01 with seed 0, −0.11 with seed 1). The paper's test scenes show none with seed 0 and lose −0.12 with seed 1 (p = 0.002).

v2 against the round 1 model, new inland holdouts. On the 32 tiles of the Yemen highlands (near) and Bangladesh (far), v2 gains +0.08 (seed 0) and +0.03 (seed 1). Neither is significant (p = 0.79 and 0.39). Yemen leans positive (+0.23 and +0.18, p = 0.06 and 0.40). Bangladesh leans negative (−0.07 and −0.11; seed 1 p = 0.016). The round 1 model, trained without any inland ground, already does about as well on these holdouts as on the old ones. It beats bicubic by 5.2 SSIM points on inland tiles and 6.3 on the old ones.

By land-cover class. v2 gains about 0.4 points on bare and sparse ground (41.4 to 41.8) and grassland (43.0 to 43.4), and 0.2 on cropland. It loses about 0.2 on tree cover and 0.1 on built-up. The added data was mostly bare ground, so the gains follow the training mix, and they stay small next to the lead every ESRT holds over bicubic: from 2.0 points on built-up ground to 11.6 on cropland.

Change mask. Masking IW-detected change (7.2% of training loss pixels) does not help. Against the same-seed unmasked model it changes SSIM by −0.05 on the old holdouts (p < 0.001), −0.16 in Houston (4% of tiles better), +0.08 in Western Australia and 0.00 on the inland holdouts (p = 0.79). Most flagged pixels sit near the speckle floor rather than at real change, and the IW-to-Stripmap gap is under 6 days. The mask removes little that misleads the model.

What else changed. Measured in homogeneous windows, v2 models despeckle less than the round 1 model: ENL on the pilot holdouts falls from about 190 to about 95 (Western Australia 297 to 127). Their output detail is slightly closer to Stripmap's (HF ratio 0.21 against 0.20), and SSIM is unchanged. Detail correlation with Stripmap stays at 0.087 against 0.086, so the extra data does not make the 5 m detail more real.

Verdict. The round 1 conclusion holds. The IW-to-Stripmap model generalizes across land cover about as well from 10 mostly coastal training regions as from 21 that add inland ground. Its gains over bicubic come from despeckling, not from new 5 m detail. The change mask can stay off.

Validation SSIM (Lee, 63 v2 validation tiles): ESRT, round 1 set (v1) 0.4124, ESRT, v2 + change mask 0.4168, ESRT, v2, no mask 0.4170, ESRT, v2, no mask, seed 1 0.4164.
