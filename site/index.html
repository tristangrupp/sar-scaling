Can open SAR sharpen Sentinel-1?

A running log of experiments on super-resolving Sentinel-1 radar using open X-band satellite data (Capella, Umbra, ICEYE) and Sentinel-1's own Stripmap mode. Each page below is self-contained and rebuilt from the project's data with build_site.py.

2
Sentinel-1 IW → Stripmap at 5 m, X-band for geometry only negative for X-band

Arm A (the published ESRT recipe, reproduced) beats bicubic on every held-out group, mostly by removing speckle. Arm B, which adds an X-band edge loss, scores slightly worse on held-out Stripmap (SSIM −0.21, p < 0.001), so X-band stays a validation reference only. Adding 25 inland scenes and a change mask (round 3) barely moves held-out scores; gains over bicubic come from despeckling.

NISAR L-band is the best open training source: same-instant pairs at two bandwidths. A tuned model beats bicubic on all five far-holdout metrics; an X-band edge loss again adds nothing.

4
Despeckling: cleaner, more interpretable Sentinel-1 done

A VV+VH Noise2Noise CNN trained on 24 sites, with no clean target, is the best single-date despeckler (about 0.7 dB above Lee) and lifts land-cover accuracy from 0.770 to 0.806. It still trails Lee in textured desert and does not help field boundaries at 10 m.

5
Cross-band transfer: does an X-band-trained model work on C-band? running

An X-band-only model beats bicubic on real Stripmap zero-shot, and pretraining helps short L1 runs. But matched to the published recipe (round 4: ESRT and CNN, SSIM loss, 8,000 steps, 3 seeds), neither X-band nor X + L pretraining beats training from scratch beyond seed noise: SSIM 41.6–41.8 for every variant, 0 of 30 tests pass.

Where things stand

X-band fails as a direct training target: as a brightness target (experiment 1) and as an edge loss (experiments 2 and 3) it adds nothing or makes held-out Stripmap worse. Real Stripmap sits no closer to X-band edges than bicubic IW does, so at 5 m X-band edges are a weak reference for C-band detail. As pretraining, X-band transfers real knowledge (zero-shot it beats bicubic, and it speeds up short training), but with the full published recipe and 200 real pairs it does not improve the final Stripmap model (experiment 5, round 4). So the open question this project set out to test, whether Capella-style X-band beats the published IW to Stripmap baseline, comes back no at this data size. What works is same-sensor supervision (IW to Stripmap, NISAR bandwidth pairs) and despeckling, where learned models beat classic filters and help a downstream map.

What changed after experiment 1

Experiment 1 asked a 10 m (about 20 m true resolution) C-band input to produce 2.5 m X-band. That is an 8x resolution jump across two wavelengths, scored against images taken from different look directions. The models learned to remove speckle and blur; detail they added did not match real detail.

Experiment 2 keeps the band fixed: Sentinel-1 IW to Sentinel-1 Stripmap, about 4x in true resolution. X-band only checks where edges sit, never how bright things are.
