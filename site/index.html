Can open SAR sharpen Sentinel-1?

Experiments on sharpening Sentinel-1 radar images and reducing speckle with open X-band data (Capella, Umbra and ICEYE), Sentinel-1 Stripmap and NISAR.

Experiment log

0
Overlap atlas done

Open X-band imagery that overlaps Sentinel-1 Stripmap. The atlas found 1,069 candidate Umbra pairs at 12 sites, 211 ICEYE pairs at 7 sites and 110 Capella pairs at 4 sites. Most Capella imagery is HH, which limits matches with Sentinel-1 VV/VH.

1
Predicting 2.5 m X-band from Sentinel-1 IW negative

Detail match 0.04 (close to zero); fine-detail energy 5% of the real X-band. The models mainly smooth the input.

1b
Comparing two real X-band images done

Two real X-band images of the same place often score poorly too. Viewing angle and alignment affect the result.

2
Sentinel-1 IW → Stripmap at 5 m, X-band for geometry only negative for X-band

The baseline adapted from the published ESRT method beat bicubic, mostly by reducing speckle. Adding X-band edge loss reduced held-out SSIM by 0.21 points (p < 0.001). More inland data and a change mask made little difference in round 3.

3
NISAR bandwidth pairs done

NISAR provides low- and high-bandwidth images from the same acquisition. The tuned model beat bicubic on all five metrics in the distant test regions. X-band edge loss did not help; GAN fine-tuning added texture but reduced detail correlation.

4
Despeckling: cleaner, more interpretable Sentinel-1 done

A VV+VH Noise2Noise model trained on 24 sites gained about 0.7 dB PSNR over Lee. WorldCover classification accuracy rose from 0.770 on raw images to 0.806. It still performed poorly on textured desert and did not improve field-boundary detection.

5
Pretraining across radar bands done

An X-band-only model beat bicubic against Stripmap references, and pretraining helped short runs. With 200 real training tiles and the full 8,000-step recipe, none of 40 comparisons showed an improvement beyond seed variation.

6
Field-boundary labels drawn on Capella planned

Designed, not run. It would correct field boundaries on sub-meter Capella images and test whether they train a better Sentinel-1 boundary model. It needs 20 to 40 hours of human review.

7
How small a change can Sentinel-1 catch? spot check

Capella repeat pairs served as automatic change truth at 33 sites. Sentinel-1 VV caught about half of change patches near 900 m², almost none under 16 m², and never reached 90%. About 7% of stable pixels were flagged.

8
How narrow a feature can Sentinel-1 show? spot check

On 803 road, track, ditch and field-edge segments measured on Capella, a multi-date Sentinel-1 mean showed about half at 5.5 m width. Width mattered less than expected, and detection never reached 90%.

9
Sentinel-1 flag clusters as Capella tasking cues spot check

A rule tuned on half the sites (VV flags in clusters of 2 ha or more) found Capella change in 89% of 500 m looks on the held-out half, against 75% for random looks. The lift held formally but rests on 5 sites, and the brightest change pixels did as well without clustering.

Where things stand

X-band data did not improve the final Sentinel-1 model in these tests. Direct image targets and edge guidance did not help. Pretraining helped short runs, but the benefit disappeared with the full training schedule. Training against images from the same sensor and reducing speckle gave the clearest gains.

What changed after experiment 1

Experiment 1 tried to predict 2.5 m X-band imagery from Sentinel-1 IW, which has a 10 m pixel grid and about 20 m spatial resolution. The images also differed in wavelength and viewing angle. The models smoothed the input without recovering the fine structure visible in X-band.

Experiment 2 used 5 m Sentinel-1 Stripmap as the reference, keeping the radar band fixed. It then tested whether adding X-band edge guidance improved the result.
