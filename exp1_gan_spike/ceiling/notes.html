Interpretation

Fine-detail agreement depends strongly on viewing geometry and alignment. Nearly repeated views aligned within a meter or two reach detail correlation of 0.45–0.63. Other pairs score about 0.1–0.25, falling below 0.1 when geometry differs substantially.

The metric is sensitive to alignment: shifting an image by 2.5 m reduces its self-correlation to 0.37; a 5 m shift reduces it to 0.11. The median raw offset between acquisitions was 40 m, with 64% exceeding 20 m. Registration required a coarse 10 m step followed by refinement at 2.5 m.

Outside nearly repeated views, blurring X-band to 10 m gives similar correlation (0.12–0.18) to using the full-resolution image. Much of the shared signal is therefore in broader edges rather than repeatable 2.5 m texture.

Experiment 1 used 10 m alignment and images with different viewing geometry. These repeat-image results suggest that correlations around 0.1–0.2 are a more useful reference than 0.6–0.7 for that setup, but they do not establish a strict upper bound. The model’s correlation near 0.05 and low fine-detail energy still show poor recovery. Finer registration, repeated viewing geometry and edge-distance metrics would make a stronger test.
